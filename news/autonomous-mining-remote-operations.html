Autonomous haulage and remote operations: the new mine

How autonomous haul trucks, drones, digital twins and remote operations centres are changing mining productivity, safety and sustainability.

Insight · Mining & Minerals · By the Future Markets Technology editorial team

A different kind of mine site

At leading operations, haul trucks now drive themselves along pit roads, drills position and operate with minimal human input, and entire sites are supervised from remote operations centres that may be hundreds of kilometres away. Mining companies in Australia were among the early adopters, and the approach is spreading worldwide.

Why miners automate

Automation keeps people out of hazardous areas, makes operations more predictable and allows equipment to run for more hours. It also helps address skills shortages in remote regions. Consistent driving and operating behaviour can reduce fuel use and wear on tyres and components.

The technology stack

Autonomous haulage relies on precise positioning, obstacle detection and fleet management software. Drones survey pits and stockpiles in hours instead of days. Digital twins bring geology, equipment and production data into a single model so planners can test decisions before they are made on site.

The next wave

Electrification of mine fleets, sensor-based ore sorting and water recycling are moving up the agenda as miners respond to climate targets and demand for critical minerals such as copper and lithium.

Opportunities for technology companies

Suppliers of sensors, connectivity, software, electrification and processing technology have a growing market, especially where they can integrate with existing fleets and prove safety performance.
